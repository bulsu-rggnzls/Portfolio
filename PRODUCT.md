# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Hiring managers, tech leads, and recruiters evaluating frontend developers. They are scanning quickly, comparing candidates, and looking for evidence of craft, not just competence. Primary audience: senior engineers and engineering managers at product-driven companies. Secondary audience: freelance clients and collaborators.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that demonstrates frontend engineering skill through its own execution. The site IS the proof of competence — every component, animation, and typographic choice is evidence of craft. Success means a visitor leaves believing this developer builds things that feel considered, not assembled.

## Positioning

A frontend developer who builds interfaces that feel alive — where motion, typography, and spacing are treated as engineering problems, not afterthoughts.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: View Projects
- Secondary CTA: Download Resume / Contact
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "This person doesn't just write code — they design experiences."
- Belief ladder: (1) The site itself is well-crafted → (2) The projects show real engineering depth → (3) The code quality matches the visual quality → (4) This person would elevate our team
- Proof on hand: project case studies with live demos and source code

## Brand Personality

Precise, confident, quietly distinctive. Not loud or flashy — the craft speaks through details. Think: an architect's monograph, not a billboard. Three words: precise, alive, considered.

## Anti-references

Generic Bootstrap portfolios, dark-mode-only developer sites with neon accents, card-grid layouts with identical project tiles, sites that look like Tailwind UI templates, anything with a "hero section with particles"

## Design Principles

1. **The site is the portfolio** — every design decision is a demonstration of skill
2. **Motion reveals character** — animations serve hierarchy and feedback, never decoration
3. **Typography is the primary visual element** — images support, type leads
4. **Restraint is not timidity** — deliberate choices, not defaults
5. **Details compound** — micro-interactions, hover states, and spacing consistency build trust

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA compliance. Reduced motion support. Keyboard navigable. Focus indicators visible. Touch targets ≥44px. Body text minimum 16px.
